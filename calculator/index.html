How much CRA time do your engineers really have?

A regulation's deadline is not your engineers' deadline. Requirements pass through handoffs, wait on other teams, compete for people's time, and run into freezes, releases and audits. Add the constraints your organization actually has to see the real engineering window before the EU Cyber Resilience Act applies in full, whether the work fits, and when it must start.

    If you miss the deadline

    —Maximum CRA fine for non-compliance with the essential requirements: €15M or 2.5% of worldwide turnover, whichever is higher
    —Estimated time past the deadline before the work is done
    —EU sales at risk while the product can't be placed on the EU market

    Fines are maximums; authorities weigh the size of the company, the severity and the duration. A non-compliant product can also be ordered off the EU market. Estimates only, not legal advice.

    What the CRA says about company size

      How it works. The requirement reaches engineers after the handoffs (steps marked as parallel take as long as the longest of them), or after the standards date if that is later. Work starts at the next planning cycle unless mid-cycle entry is allowed, and no earlier than the longest dependency allows. The real deadline is the regulatory deadline minus code-complete lead time and evidence preparation, moved earlier if it falls inside a blocked period. Blocked days inside the working window are subtracted, and the remaining weeks are multiplied by the share of time available. To find when work must start, the calculator counts back from the real deadline by the work needed at that capacity, skipping blocked days, then subtracts the dependency wait and the handoffs.
